# T5: Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer

**Every NLP task — classification, QA, translation, summarisation, regression — can be cast as "text in, text out", and once you do, a single controlled empirical survey of the whole transfer-learning design space (objective, architecture, data, scale) becomes possible with one model, one loss, one set of hyperparameters.**

## Summary

Raffel et al. (Google, 2019/JMLR 2020) unify NLP under a text-to-text interface (task signalled by a text prefix), then run a large one-axis-at-a-time ablation to isolate the effect of each transfer-learning choice. Their findings: encoder-decoder with a span-corruption denoising objective is the most robust recipe (decoder-only LM as a pre-training objective is markedly worse in this setting); among denoising variants the exact details matter surprisingly little (a broad plateau); data quality matters (cleaning Common Crawl into the released **C4** corpus beats the raw dump); and, at fixed compute, scaling the model and training longer are dependable levers. This is BACKGROUND/foundational — its lasting value is methodological (text-to-text framing + controlled ablation) more than any single number, and its scale-first conclusions cut against the sub-1B direction.

## Why it matters here

Two threads for a trust-and-empathy on-device thesis: (1) the **text-to-text framing** is the conceptual ancestor of casting every downstream behaviour (personalisation, empathetic response, tool-use) as conditional text generation with a task prefix — useful when explaining why one small model can be steered to many behaviours by prompting; (2) the **controlled-ablation discipline** is a template for the project's own SFT ablations. Cite as the origin of the framework and the survey method, and note explicitly that the on-device regime *inverts* several of T5's scale-first conclusions.

## Method / key findings

- **Text-to-text:** all tasks share one max-likelihood objective and decoding; classification labels, regression targets, spans, translations all emitted as text.
- **C4:** ~750 GB of heuristically-cleaned English Common Crawl (terminal-punctuation lines, ≥3 sentences, deduped, English-prob ≥0.99, blocklist filtered).
- **Baseline (~220M encoder-decoder):** GLUE 83.28, SQuAD 80.88, SuperGLUE 71.36, WMT En-De 26.98 BLEU. Ablating pre-training entirely drops GLUE to 66.22 / SQuAD to 50.31 (pre-training ≈ +17 GLUE / +30 SQuAD).
- **Architecture:** full encoder-decoder + denoising best; decoder-only LM clearly worst (GLUE 74.70); prefix-LM in between.

> Note: the digesting pass could not reliably render the large-scale T5-3B/11B leaderboard tables, so this page deliberately quotes only the verified ~220M baseline/architecture numbers and does *not* cite specific 11B figures — confirm those against the source (JMLR 2020) before using them.

## Critical appraisal

Landmark and unusually honest — a survey dressed as an experiments paper; the text-to-text interface directly anticipated instruction-following and prompt-based LLMs. Weaknesses: English-only, compute-maximalist, a "systematic survey" only as general as its fixed 2019 baseline; "coordinate ascent" ablation can miss interaction effects; and it engages with neither trust/calibration nor small-model/on-device constraints — its notion of "better" is benchmark average. The C4 cleaning heuristics also bake in content biases (blocklist filtering can strip minority dialects) that later work criticised.
