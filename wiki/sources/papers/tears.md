# TEARS: Textual Representations for Scrutable Recommendations

**Replace opaque latent user embeddings with human-readable natural-language preference summaries (LLM-generated), align the summary encoding to a strong black-box VAE recommender via optimal transport, and let users edit the text to steer recommendations — delivering scrutability and controllability while matching or beating the black-box baselines' accuracy.**

## Summary

Penaloza, Gouvert, Wu and Charlin (Mila / UdeM / McGill / HEC, 2024/2025) build a scrutable recommender around a ~170-word text profile (Inferred Preferences / High-Level Attributes / Fine-Grained Details). A T5-base text encoder and a VAE history encoder produce Gaussian embeddings aligned by a 2-Wasserstein loss, mixed by a user-tunable convex weight α; editing the summary propagates to rankings with no retraining. Aligned TEARS variants *statistically beat* their VAE backbones on several dataset/metric pairs (ML-1M Recall@20 0.323 vs 0.301), while the text-only ablation collapses to 0.031 Recall@20 — the load-bearing evidence that a natural-language profile must be fused with a collaborative signal. This is the canonical scrutable/editable-profile reference, with a clear "shrink to on-device + add privacy" gap for the thesis.

## Why it matters here

The strongest existing evidence that representing the user as *natural-language text the user can read and edit* is viable and even accuracy-positive — direct support for a scrutable [[entities/5w-h|5W+H]] profile as the on-device personalisation substrate, with its three-part summary schema as a ready template. Its controllability benchmarks (large-scope / fine-grained / guided edits, measured by rank movement) are a reusable design for testing whether edits to a 0.6B model's scrutable profile change behaviour, and the tunable α (text vs black-box weight) is a control analogue of deciding how much a stored preference should influence output ([[sources/papers/rpeval|Ignore/Support/Dominate]]).

## Method

- **Two encoders:** T5-base (+LoRA) on the LLM summary → `Z_s`; a VAE on interaction history → `Z_r`.
- **Optimal-transport alignment:** minimise the 2-Wasserstein distance between the two Gaussian spaces.
- **Head:** convex mix `Z_c = α·Z_s + (1−α)·Z_r` (α=0.5 in training, user-adjustable at inference).
- **Summaries** by GPT-4-turbo / LLaMA 3.1 (~168–180 words). Datasets: MovieLens-1M, Netflix, Goodbooks.

## Key results

- **Accuracy (statistically significant, small absolute):** ML-1M TEARS-MacridVAE Recall@20 0.323 vs 0.301; Netflix 0.518 vs 0.515.
- **Text-only collapses:** 0.031 Recall@20 on ML-1M — alignment with the collaborative signal is essential.
- **Edits steer output:** coarse (genre flip) and fine (plot edit) edits move rankings in the intended direction; α is a transparency/performance dial.

## Critical appraisal

The cleanest demonstration that a textual, editable profile can be a recommender's control surface without sacrificing accuracy, with an elegant optimal-transport trick and genuine controllability benchmarks. Cautions: accuracy gains are small in absolute terms (the real win is scrutability); summaries are generated by *large* cloud LLMs and the T5+VAE stack is server-scale; no real user study (controllability is rank movement, not human perception); privacy is not addressed (summaries generated centrally from full histories).

> ⚠ 0.6B / privacy caution: TEARS relies on large LLMs to *write* summaries and a server-side stack — the thesis must show a **0.6B on-device** model can both generate and consume scrutable summaries, which TEARS does not attempt; the text-only 0.031 Recall@20 warns a pure-text on-device profile without a collaborative signal may be weak; and it addresses neither privacy nor forgetting, so it *complements* the privacy-aware-memory papers rather than substituting for them.
