# Meeting Notes — September 2025

**The first advisor–student meeting establishing the research domain as human-AI interaction, with scrutability and explainability as the organising concepts, and the "Inside Out" multi-agent AI as an early directional hypothesis.**

## Summary

Owen Conlan (supervisor, Room F29 O'Reilly Building) introduced Ajinkya to the project structure, emphasising literature review and clear research questions as the primary obligations of a dissertation. Owen's own research interest is *scrutability* — the user's ability to understand not just what an AI system does but why, and to correct it when wrong. He described current LLMs as "sociopaths" in the explanation sense: they produce statistically plausible responses but cannot expose their internal logical structure. The meeting ended with Owen suggesting the movie "Inside Out" as loose inspiration for a multi-agent AI where competing emotional subsystems collaborate or contest to produce responses, and with the agreement that the topic is not fixed and will evolve through literature review.

## Key concepts raised

**Scrutability vs explainability.** Owen distinguished scrutability (user can understand and control the system) from explainability (the system can narrate what it did). The distinction is important: a system can produce explanations post-hoc while still being opaque. The research target is scrutability — the user has genuine agency.

**LLMs as "sociopaths."** Owen's analogy: LLMs are convincing but have no genuine understanding or introspective access to their own reasoning. They pattern-match on statistics and will answer anything without admitting limitation. This framing motivates the need for architectural mechanisms (not just prompting) to produce trustworthy outputs.

**Inside Out AI.** Owen suggested exploring whether competing emotional/cognitive subsystems (modelled after the film's characters) could serve as a grounded multi-agent architecture. He was careful to say the film is a motivational frame, not a blueprint — the architecture must find its own evidence-based justification in the literature.

**Empathetic Dialogue dataset.** Owen demonstrated a dataset of empathetic conversational exchanges and noted both the potential and the limitations: empathy expressed in words can be apathetic in tone, and such datasets may teach surface patterns rather than genuine responsiveness. This flags the need for appraisal-theoretic grounding rather than surface-level affect mirroring.
